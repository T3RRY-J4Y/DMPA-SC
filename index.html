What is DMPA-SC?

A new injectable contraceptive that is safe, effective and easy to use. A healthcare provider gives your first injection and shows you how to do the next one, then you can take a unit home to self-inject after 3 months.

Not comfortable injecting yourself? A health provider can do it for you.

  • 99%effective at preventing pregnancy
  • 3 monthsof protection from one injection
  • Just under the skinInjected subcutaneously, not into the muscle, with a small, short needle, often less painful.
  • Fewer clinic visitsOnly every 6 months for refills if you self-inject.
  • Control & privacyFits into your schedule and lifestyle, at home.

How to self-inject

The MAPS method

Four simple steps. Inject in your abdomen (away from the navel) or the front of your thigh.

Step 1 of 4

Step 1: M – Mix & check

  1. Wash your hands and gather your supplies.
  2. Open the pouch and remove the device.
  3. Shake the device for about 30 seconds.
  4. Check for any damage or leaks.

Step 2: A – Activate

  1. Hold the device by the port, with the needle pointing upward.
  2. Push the needle cap firmly into the port to close the gap. This activates the device.
  3. Remove the needle cap.

Step 3: P – Pinch & insert

  1. Choose an injection site: abdomen (away from the navel) or front of thigh. Avoid scars, moles or bruises.
  2. Clean the area if visibly dirty. Pinch the skin to create a “tent”.
  3. Insert the needle at a downward angle until the port touches the skin fully.

Step 4: S – Slowly press

  1. Press the reservoir slowly for 5–7 seconds until it is flat.
  2. Remove the needle, then let go of the skin. Apply gentle pressure if needed. Don’t rub.
  3. It’s okay if a small amount of liquid remains in the device.

Do not put the needle cap back on.

After you're done

Place the used device straight into a puncture-proof container with a tight lid, like a plastic bottle or jar. Dispose of it in a rubbish bin, or return it to a clinic when you can.

What to expect after your injection

Everyone's body is different, and it's normal to notice some changes. Most side effects are mild and go away over time.

Your period may change

Lighter or heavier bleeding, spotting, or your period may stop. This is safe and common. It does not mean you are pregnant or unwell.

Small body changes

Some women notice mild headaches or small changes in weight. These are usually temporary.

A sore injection spot

Slight pain, redness or swelling where you injected should disappear in a few days.

Mood or energy shifts

If changes in mood or desire bother you, talk to your nurse or health worker. Small adjustments can help.

Remember: these effects are normal while your body adjusts. If you have heavy bleeding or anything feels worrying, contact your clinic. You're never alone in this.

Remember your next injection

  • Inject every 3 months to keep preventing pregnancy.
  • You have a window: up to 2 weeks early or up to 4 weeks late is fine.
  • Write the date on your reminder card or phone calendar before leaving the clinic.
  • Keep your next unit somewhere safe and easy to remember, like your toiletry bag or with your sanitary pads.
  • More than 4 weeks late? Don't inject. Use condoms and contact your clinic.

Do not inject if… you are seriously ill, or the solution looks cloudy, discoloured or is expired. Never share your injection device.
Does it hurt?

DMPA-SC uses a small, short needle injected just under the skin, so it is often less painful than other injectables. You may feel slight soreness at the spot for a few days.

Does DMPA-SC protect against HIV or STIs?

No. Use condoms as well if you need protection against HIV and STIs.

What if I miss my injection date?

You can still inject up to 4 weeks after your due date. If you are later than that, don't inject: use condoms and contact your clinic as soon as possible, or WhatsApp us on 078 168 0192.

Can I stop or switch methods?

Yes, at any time. Talk to your nurse about what works best for you. DMPA-SC prevents pregnancy for 3 months after your last injection; fertility returns after that, though it may take a few months.

What if I don’t want to inject myself?

That’s okay. A health provider can give you the injection at the clinic every 3 months.

Where can I get it?

Contraceptives are free at your local public clinic or hospital. Your first injection is given by a provider, who will train you to do the next one.
